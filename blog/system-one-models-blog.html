System One Models Are Real —
We Built One 9 Months Ago

TypeSafe AI just validated a category ConceptNet has been building since 2025. Here's what we learned — and why the enterprise version looks very different.

By Tony Momoh · Founder, ConceptNet Ltd · September 21, 2026 · 5 min read

On September 15, 2026, TypeSafe AI came out of stealth with Jev — a model that returns typed decisions instead of generating text. LangChain called it the missing piece for agent loops. The AI world called it a new category: System One models.

We built one 9 months ago. Before anyone had a name for it.

The problem we saw first

Every enterprise AI agent needs to classify what kind of action a command requires before it executes. Not what to do — when and how.

Execute now? Wait for a trigger? Act before something happens? Run forever automatically? Those are four completely different execution types. An LLM making that decision on every query is like using a power station to light a single candle. It works. But it costs 400× too much and takes 200× too long.

We built ConceptNet to solve exactly this — a dedicated classifier for enterprise intent. Four deterministic outputs. No text generation. No hallucination possible. Just a structured decision your agent can act on immediately.

What independent verification actually looks like

Before TypeSafe launched. Before LangChain published. An independent ML researcher from the Hugging Face community found ConceptNet and tried to break it.

He used grouped lexical-family holdout splits — specifically designed to catch train/test leakage and memorisation. He confirmed 99.3% on the adversarial holdout. We fixed 4 issues in 24 hours. Retrained to 100%.

"The obvious train/test leakage explanation did not survive that check." — Independent researcher, Hugging Face community

That's peer review. Not a benchmark. Not a marketing claim. Someone tried to break it and couldn't.

Why enterprise needs something different

Jev is designed for software that knows its possible answers in advance — support ticket routing, lead scoring, trust and safety checks. Those are important problems.

Enterprise workflow automation has an additional requirement: the classification layer must work in environments where the internet doesn't. Government data centres. Military installations. Hospitals with strict data sovereignty requirements. Companies in markets where US AI infrastructure is restricted or politically unacceptable.

ConceptNet runs entirely locally. No API call. No cloud. No data leaving the device. Sub-5ms on CPU hardware. That's not a feature for enterprise. That's a procurement requirement.

The category Jev validated

We're genuinely pleased TypeSafe AI launched. Not because it validates our business — though it does. Because it proves the category is real.

The next two years will see dedicated classification models replace LLMs for every bounded decision task in enterprise software. Intent classification is the first and largest of those tasks. Every AI agent makes this decision thousands of times a day.

The question isn't whether System One models are real. LangChain just published 1,500 words answering that.

The question is which one you trust with your enterprise workflows, your government data, and your autonomous systems.
